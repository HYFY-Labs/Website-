01The world

Let's Glow.

Cosmic Baseball packs the house. Neon night, full gate, merch moving all game. Then the lights go down, everyone walks out, and the team never hears from any of them again.

  • A sellout tells you how many came. It never tells you who.
  • Merch revenue lands once. The relationship ends at the register.
  • Next season you buy the same fans back through ads, from platforms that already know them better than you do.

02The product moment

One Tap.

A connected chip goes into the cap and the tee. A fan holds their phone against it and the glow goes digital. No app to download, no scanner at the gate, nobody standing in a line to opt in.

  • Embedded in the garment, not a hangtag that gets cut off and thrown away in the parking lot.
  • Works on any modern phone. Tap and go, the same gesture as paying for a beer.
  • One tap is the entire ask. Everything else happens after you already have them.
03

03Connect and celebrate

Unlock The Night.

The tap opens a page built for that game, on your brand, in your voice. Content nobody else gets, a quest that runs across the season, an entry in the ticket lottery, and a fan profile that remembers all of it.

  • Rewards for coming back, not for spending more. The fan earns their way deeper into the club.
  • Ties straight into the drop. The merch becomes the entry, so the cap sells the ticket and the ticket sells the cap.
  • The page is yours. Your art, your promos, changed between homestands without reprinting anything.

04The thesis

Your Fans, Finally Mapped.

Every tap writes a record you own. Who they are, what they bought, when they showed up, and how to reach them on a Tuesday in February. That is the product. The hat carries it.

  • First party, so it survives whatever the ad platforms decide to do next.
  • A direct channel back to every fan, with nobody charging you rent to reach the people already wearing your logo.
  • It stays with the team. Not a vendor's list, not a marketplace's audience. Yours.

05The receipts

We Already Ran It.

Hamel Hawks, 100th anniversary weekend. Connected merch on the floor, fans tapping in the stands, and a fan database that did not exist on Friday morning.

  • A real club, not a lab. Town ball, one weekend, the same operational reality you run every homestand.
  • Those are the four measures, not the results. The Hamel figures go onto that board the day the pilot export is final, and not a day before.

06Scale

One Platform. Every Team.

The same layer runs for any club selling merch to people it cannot name. Town ball, indie pro, summer collegiate, college. One platform underneath all of it, and one place the fan data lands.

  • You run the game. We run the connected layer, the chips, the unlock pages and the data plumbing.
  • It rides the merch you already sell. No new product line, no new vendor for your fans to learn.
  • Every club keeps its own data. Nothing pools into a shared list you have to share with the team down the road.
